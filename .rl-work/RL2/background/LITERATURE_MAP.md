# Literature map

Research cutoff: 2026-09-30. Session: RL2. Status: NOT PROMOTED — working research checkpoint.

Ordinary Hadwiger asserts that every finite simple graph satisfies h(G) >= chi(G), equivalently that chi(G) >= t forces a K_t minor for every integer t. A model has t nonempty, disjoint, connected branch sets with every pair adjacent. This exact simultaneous conclusion is the reference point for all comparisons. [RES-0001, RES-0002; TOP-0001]

The 1943 origin is cataloged without pretending the original was directly inspected. Contemporary primary papers retain the open unrestricted conjecture. Cases through t=6 are established; t=5 and t=6 connect to the Four Color Theorem. Robertson–Seymour–Thomas's t=6 work supplies a fixed-parameter apex reduction for a minor-minimal counterexample. This is an actual bridge at its stated scope, not a uniform apex theorem. Ordinary Hadwiger also holds for line graphs of loopless multigraphs. [RES-0003–RES-0007; SRC-0001–SRC-0004, SRC-0024; TOP-0002]

The asymptotic literature separates density from coloring. The Kostochka–Thomason density scale gives degeneracy of order t sqrt(log t), and that order is tight for degeneracy. Norin–Postle–Song then broke the degeneracy barrier for coloring: every exponent beta > 1/4 is allowed in their bound. Delcourt–Postle's checked theorem gives O(t log log t) colors. A reported September 2026 improvement is retained only as an unverified lead. None of these asymptotic statements supplies the exact t-1 colors of ordinary Hadwiger. [RES-0008–RES-0010, RES-0015; SRC-0006–SRC-0008, SRC-0011–SRC-0014; TOP-0003, TOP-0004]

Delcourt–Postle further reduce the linear weakening to graphs of order at most C t log^4 t. The parameter t stays unbounded, and the transfer retains a C^2t conclusion. They also obtain linear bounds under fixed clique-subgraph exclusion and extract small highly connected subgraphs under a density hypothesis. Norin's ICM survey explains the density-increment, connectivity, linkage and gluing toolkit. These are useful exact reductions and tools, each with a scope boundary. [RES-0011–RES-0014, RES-0028; SRC-0012, SRC-0016; TOP-0005, TOP-0006]

A January 2026 preprint links neighborhood sparsity and coloring. Its triangle-free bound has a maximum-degree restriction. Its counterexample dichotomy allows either a very large complete bipartite subgraph or a dense small subgraph in one neighborhood. The dichotomy does not exclude either alternative. [RES-0029, RES-0030; SRC-0018]

Independence and fractional coloring form a separate set of relaxations. Reed–Seymour establish the fractional factor-two bound and a weighted stable-set theorem. Duchet–Meyniel supply a bound in terms of independence number, verified here through Seymour's survey. Almost-everywhere Hadwiger concerns a specified random model. These do not provide integral sharp coloring for every graph. [RES-0019–RES-0022; SRC-0005, SRC-0009, SRC-0015, SRC-0021; TOP-0008, TOP-0010]

Strengthenings can fail. Hajós' subdivision strengthening is false; list-coloring analogues have explicit counterexamples. The December 2025 odd-minor preprint disproves the sharp odd strengthening, including a strong obstruction in independence-number-two graphs. Ordinary Hadwiger remains distinct. Steiner's factor-two transfer connects ordinary and odd coloring at the asymptotic scale, not at the sharp scale. [RES-0016–RES-0018, RES-0023–RES-0025; SRC-0010, SRC-0013, SRC-0017; TOP-0009, TOP-0012, TOP-0013]

The Four Color Theorem is a useful verification precedent: finite reducibility work is accompanied by global unavoidability. Gonthier's formalization is theorem-specific. Neither an inherited computation nor a formalization proves general Hadwiger. Alternative fixed target minors in the H-Hadwiger family give weaker instances; the complete-target instance recovers the root conjecture. [RES-0026, RES-0027, RES-0031; TOP-0007, TOP-0011, TOP-0014]

Historical titles can hide indexing differences: Seymour's HC(s) means our minor-order t=s+1. “K6-free” in the 1993 paper is minor exclusion. Density d=e/v is half average degree. Source and glossary records preserve these distinctions and version changes so later sessions do not rediscover or silently mistranslate them.
