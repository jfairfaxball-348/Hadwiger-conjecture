# Every tenth session: progress and correction audit

Authorization: direct user instruction, 2026-10-01 Europe/Madrid, during
RL9 closeout: install RL10 as a progress/correction audit and make every
tenth session review the preceding ten sessions to avoid the recorded
Collatz failure patterns. This operational policy adds no mathematical
claim and does not alter frozen history, certificates or source status.

## Cadence and inventory

Every RL numbered 10, 20, 30, and so on is an audit session. Its sole
incoming brief must schedule a bounded review of the ten preceding session
positions and current authority before further theorem discovery. Thus
RL20 reviews RL10–RL19; RL30 reviews RL20–RL29. Audit sessions themselves
count in later windows. No periodic external automation is involved.

At the first audit, the current inventory has RL1–RL9 and an initialization
baseline, with no numbered RL0. RL10 must verify that inventory, review
RL1–RL9 and separately inspect the preserved initialization/scaffold
baseline. Identify the ten intended predecessor positions and any missing
session record explicitly. Do not invent RL0, treat initialization as a
completed research session without evidence, or hide a shortfall. Follow
older provenance only where a live dependency requires it.

## Required review

Keep full sharp Hadwiger, h(G)>=chi(G) for every finite simple graph, as
the root. Either a complete rigorous proof or an actual rigorously verified
finite h<chi graph is legitimate. Review each session's input, result,
exact quantifiers, proof classification, verifier scope, corrections,
failures, lessons, and precise contribution to a named root obligation.
Assess valid local results separately from applicability to the root.

Recheck load-bearing proofs and hypotheses at their consuming scopes.
Challenge universal-coloring claims, critical/auxiliary distinctions,
independence and extraction assumptions, coverage and sharpness, and
simultaneous branch models. Audit all unbounded residual parameters.
Finite witnesses and packaging verifiers cannot certify analytic universal
claims. Source-access failures do not establish openness. Do not use
session counts, confidence percentages or accumulated notes as progress.

Apply the inherited Collatz review: test local-to-global sufficiency,
renamed/equivalent unsolved bridges, finite signatures with unbounded
residuals, application without hypotheses, unsupported numerical/model
consequences, and repeated local work without obligation closure. A
same-worker fresh proof review is not external independent certification.

## Outputs and correction gate

Produce an evidence-linked session matrix; a load-bearing dependency and
scope audit; a Collatz-risk assessment; an explicit correction/demotion
record (including none if justified); and a verdict to continue, repair,
pivot or pause the current route. Preserve every valid restricted result.
For each defect identify the first invalid inference, surviving frontier,
affected downstream claims, correction evidence and retry conditions.
Freeze dependent deductions immediately under the recovery protocol;
never erase the original mistake or amend frozen history.

End with one exact bounded successor task chosen from the audited root
dependencies, with quantifiers, independent sufficiency, provenance,
falsification/stopping criteria and finite computation bounds if relevant.
Prepared candidates are options, not an automatic mandate. No new broad
proof campaign, census or unchanged source-gate replay during the audit.
Existing verifiers may run only at their recorded complete finite scopes;
any new computation needs complete bounds and a verifier first. New
source retrieval requires a named necessary claim and recorded bounds.

Use the normal start gate, proof classifications, failure ledger,
CLOSEOUT_LOCK, checkpoint and atomic numbered closeout rules. Audits do
not terminate the programme by default. A user-authorized policy amendment
is a separate non-RL process commit and supplies no mathematical progress.
